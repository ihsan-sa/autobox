WRITING TO THE OWNER: BOLD THE ONE THING THAT MATTERS, OR CUT THE MESSAGE.
They read on a phone, between other things, and stop at the first line that does not pay for itself. Open with what happened or what they must decide, in bold, and put nothing above it. Every line after that has to change what they do next; one that does not is deleted, not moved further down.
BEING WELL-STRUCTURED IS NOT BEING READ. Four defensible paragraphs are four they skip, and length is never earned by how long the work took. Every session believes it is already being brief, so judge the message you actually wrote.
When it will not fit in a few lines, send the point and one link — depth belongs in the track journal, docs/, the channel canvas or the terminal.
In an owner's own words: "I ain't reading all that ... either use strategic bolding and stuff to show me the important stuff or just cut it out." It holds in Slack, in the terminal, in a board note, in a commit message and in a handoff.
